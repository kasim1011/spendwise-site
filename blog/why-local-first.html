Why local-first: the case for an offline expense tracker

Most expense trackers are online products with an offline mode bolted on. Spendwise is the other way round: the ledger is a database on your phone and the network is optional. That is a real trade, not a slogan, so here is both sides of it.

Published: 8 November 2026  ·  Platform: Android 10 and above  ·  Price: free, supported by one non-personalised ad

What local-first means here

Every transaction, category, budget, person and receipt attachment lives in SQLite inside the app's private storage on the device. Screens read from that database. There is no login, no sync queue, and no server that has to answer before the app can show what you spent.

That is the whole architecture. "Offline support" usually means a local cache of something that lives on a server, with a reconciliation step when the connection comes back. Here there is no copy to reconcile, because there is no server copy.

What you keep

  • It works in a basement. Logging a purchase, editing a budget and exporting a report all run on the device. Airplane mode changes nothing.
  • Speed. A local query answers immediately. No spinner stands between you and the number you opened the app to check.
  • A smaller breach surface. A ledger that never leaves the phone cannot be exposed in somebody else's database. There is no account to take over and no password to reuse.
  • No tracking ID. The app does not ship an advertising identifier, and the analytics it does send carry no identifier that follows you to another app or site.

What you give up

Local-first is not free, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. The costs are concrete:

  • No live sync across devices. Your phone is the ledger. A tablet is a second, separate ledger unless you move a backup file between them.
  • No web app. There is nothing to open in a browser at work.
  • No automatic bank import. Entries are typed, or brought in from a backup file you move yourself.
  • Shared ledgers are one keeper per device. A group in Spendwise is a scope on your own database, not a live shared document. See how group expenses work before expecting four people to edit the same trip at once.
  • Losing the phone means losing anything after the last backup. This is the big one, and it gets its own section below.

What still leaves the phone

Two things, and they are worth stating plainly rather than burying in a policy page.

The app shows one native ad on the confirmation screen after a save, served by Google AdMob and non-personalised. It is the only revenue the app has. There is no second placement, no rewarded video and no interstitial on launch. Non-personalised means the ad is not chosen from a profile of you, and the app does not pass an advertising identifier to the SDK.

The app also sends anonymous usage events — that a screen was viewed, that an install opened — to Firebase. No amounts, note text, category names, person names or attachments are included. Analytics can be switched off in Settings, and the ad request still fires either way because it is what pays for the app. In the EEA and the UK, the ad request is consent-gated.

That is the honest boundary: the ledger does not leave the device, the app's telemetry and its one ad request do. If that is too much, the app is not the right tool; a spreadsheet and a password manager are a defensible answer.

Backups: the one thing that is on you

Because there is no server copy, recovery is your job. Spendwise writes a backup file that you choose the destination for — Downloads, a share target, or a folder your phone already syncs. Restoring it on a new phone brings the ledger back.

The backup file is not encrypted. It is a database file, readable by anything that can open it, so treat it like a bank statement rather than a password vault. Keep it somewhere you would keep a paper ledger: not in a public folder, not attached to an email to yourself that you later forget about. The private-expense-tracker page states the same limit rather than overselling the word "private".

Who this is not for

If you want a ledger that four people edit at the same time, or one that pulls transactions from your bank automatically, local-first is the wrong shape and no amount of polish fixes it. If you want a tracker that keeps working when the signal drops, answers instantly, and has no account to lose, it is the right one.

Spendwise is free on Google Play, offline, and needs no sign-up. The offline expense tracker guide covers what the app does in airplane mode, and the comparison with a spreadsheet is the honest case for not using one.
